How these numbers are worked out

Accuracy scores each move 0 to 100 by how much of your winning chance it kept, then averages them. 100% means every move was the engine's choice or just as good.

ACPL (average centipawn loss) is how much advantage you gave away per move compared with the best move, measured in hundredths of a pawn. Playing the top move costs 0; hanging a knight costs about 300. Roughly: under 20 is master level, 30 to 50 is a strong club player, 80+ means a few big mistakes. Lower is better.

Performance rating is the classic results-based figure: the average rating of your last 5 opponents, plus 400 × (wins − losses) ÷ games.

Accuracy-based rating only uses reviewed games. For each one it compares your ACPL with your opponent's. Using the rough rule that rating scales with e−ACPL/100, losing 20 fewer centipawns per move than a 1000-rated opponent reads as about 1000 × e0.2 ≈ 1220. Each game's ratio is capped at ×0.67 to ×1.5 so one wild game can't dominate.

Projected rating is the average of the two (or just the performance rating if nothing is reviewed yet). It estimates the level you played at recently. Five games is a small sample, so treat swings of ±100 as noise.

Import games from a PGN file

chess.com leaves games against bots (and some others) out of the data it shares, so they have to come in as a file. On chess.com: Profile → Games (the game archive), filter to vs Computer if you like, tick the games (or the select-all box), then Download. Drop the .pgn here or pick it below.

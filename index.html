How the projected rating is worked out

Performance rating is the classic results-based figure: the average rating of your last 5 opponents, plus 400 × (wins − losses) ÷ games. Win them all and it's opponents' average + 400; lose them all and it's average − 400.

Accuracy-based rating only uses the games Stockfish has reviewed. For each game it compares your average centipawn loss (ACPL, how much evaluation you gave away per move) with your opponent's. Using the rough rule that rating scales with e−ACPL/100, a game where you lost 20 fewer centipawns per move than a 1000-rated opponent reads as about 1000 × e0.2 ≈ 1220. The opponent's rating anchors the scale, so it calibrates itself to your rating pool. Each game's ratio is capped at ×0.67–×1.5 so one wild game can't dominate.

Projected rating is the average of the two (or just the performance rating if nothing is analyzed yet). It's an estimate of the level you played at recently, not a prediction chess.com will produce. Five games is a small sample, so treat swings of ±100 as noise.
